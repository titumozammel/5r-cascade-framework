## Use it today

You do not need to know the rest of HEE to use this card. Start with one situation from your own work.

**Use it when:** you already know a recurring demand exists and need to decide what should happen to it.

**Start with context:** When, what, and where is the demand? The same demand can warrant a different disposition in a different context. ([Start with your context](https://github.com/titumozammel/human-energy-economics#start-with-your-context-when-what-and-where))

**Ask:**

1. Should it exist at all? (**Remove**)
2. If yes, can it consume less? (**Reduce**) Can a simpler mechanism serve the purpose? (**Replace**)
3. Why does it keep arising? (**Re-engineer**) What must stay and be protected? (**Retain**)

**First step this week:** Pick one thing to simplify. Write what it protects, what it improves, and what it could put at risk. Then choose the smallest safe step.

**You get:** a decision for each part of the demand, a named destination for any time released, and a review date.

### Explained by example **[Illustrative]**

*An invented example, written in the first person to show the thinking. It is not a reported result.*

**The situation.** A weekly performance report has twelve sections. Three people spend a day each compiling it.

**What I would do first.** Split the report into parts. The parts have different purposes, so they may get different answers.

**What I would decide, part by part.**
- *Seven sections:* nobody can say who uses them, so use and purpose are Unknown (not evidence that there is none). A candidate to **Remove** only after the recipients are asked; any interim trial (stop for four weeks, restore if asked) follows the [trial rule](#remove).
- *Three sections:* used, but compiled by hand from a system that already holds the numbers. **Replace** the hand work with an export.
- *Two sections:* required by the board. **Retain**, with a named owner.
- *The hand collection itself:* why is it needed? Suppose the data feed never reached the report. **Re-engineer** the feed.

**Before releasing any hours.** I would write down where they go (for example, follow-up on late deliveries) and a review date. Without an explicit destination and reassignment rule, released hours may be absorbed by other demand; whether protection changes this is a proposed relationship to test.

**What I would check next cycle.** Did the hours move to the named place? Did anyone miss a section?

**Where it does not fit:** 5R decides what should happen to a demand. It does not carry out the change, release capacity, or create value by itself. If you do not yet know whether the demand is necessary, start with [OSF](https://github.com/titumozammel/operational-silence-framework#use-it-today).

**A useful next step:** before you act, check your decision against the four [Decision Point](#the-decision-point) assessments; afterwards record in a few lines what you saw, what you changed, and what you will check next cycle, then look again.

## HEE in four questions

**1. What is HEE?** Human Energy Economics (HEE) is a [practice-driven](https://github.com/titumozammel/human-energy-economics#4-core-definitions) management approach to execution and the capacity needed for tomorrow. It is a capacity lens (a way of looking at capacity) informed by economic questions about scarcity, opportunity cost, renewal, and future value. The 5R Cascade Framework (5R) is the method for choosing what should happen to a recurring demand once its necessity has been examined: Remove, Reduce, Replace, Re-engineer, or Retain.

**2. What does HEE contribute?** One connected way to ask what limits execution, what evidence supports the answer, and whether others can review the decision. It keeps two judgments separate: was the capacity spent *Justified?*, and will the next cycle’s demand be *Covered?* Whether this adds value beyond existing approaches remains open to independent testing. 5R adds a decision method in which Retain is a full outcome, not a failure.

**3. How do I apply it to my problem?** Start with the problem you can see and the decision you need to make. Context shapes the route: **Diagnose** (look for causes), **Establish** (collect missing evidence) or **Verify** (compare practice with a standard). The routes are not stages; change route as the evidence changes. Use this document when a recurring demand has been examined for necessity and its disposition must be chosen. The [HEE Core README](https://github.com/titumozammel/human-energy-economics) explains the routes and the six practitioner moves.

**4. How should I use HEE?** Use HEE to ask better questions and review decisions with your own evidence and judgment; it does not replace expert judgment. HEE is an approach to inquiry and judgment, guided by principles; its checklists support that work rather than determine the answer. Check in your own case whether the order of questions improves the decision, whether others classify the dispositions the same way, and whether released capacity becomes usable capacity. The [HEE Core README](https://github.com/titumozammel/human-energy-economics) holds the full definitions, evidence, and limits.

## The question 5R answers

A report is produced every week. A request type arrives every day. An approval, a meeting, a reconciliation recurs. Leaders ask how to do the work faster. But faster handling of work that need not exist is still work that need not exist, and a more efficient version of the wrong mechanism is still the wrong mechanism. Other recurring demand is entirely legitimate and should stay.

> **Once a recurring demand has been examined for necessity, what should happen to it: should it exist in this form, how little capacity should it consume, by what mechanism, what keeps it recurring, and what must stay?**

5R is a decision method for that choice. It is not a program for reducing activity, and it does not assume that recurring work is waste. **Retain** is a full outcome of the method, not a failure of it.

**Move the management decision upstream from activity to demand.** This is the premise and the proposed contribution, and both are open to testing. They are not findings. The five dispositions are an *order of questions*, not five mandatory actions and not mutually exclusive labels; one demand can call for several (for example, remove most of a report, retain a part, and re-engineer the feed).

## Owner view: one page

**[H] What to do with this document in ten minutes.**

1. **Name one recurring demand** (a report, a request type, an approval, a meeting) and what it costs, if recorded. If nothing is recorded, that is the first finding.
2. **Test necessity before choosing.** Is it needed, is it valuable, is the amount proportionate? Ask its users and owner. If its purpose is not recorded, treat that as **Unknown** and establish it before acting. Choose the route that fits: **Diagnose** when the cause is unclear, **Establish** when evidence is missing, **Verify** when a standard exists against which to check practice. The [routes table](#which-investigation-route) is in *Choosing a disposition*.
3. **Split it.** Different parts of one demand often have different purposes.
4. **Ask the five questions, in order, for each part.** Should it exist? If yes, can it consume less? Can its purpose be met by a simpler mechanism? Why does it keep arising? What must stay, and who protects it?
5. **Compare feasible options, and prefer a reversible first step** with a stop rule written before it starts. Keep any safeguard unless a replacement is shown, and name what takes over its protective purpose.
6. **Before capacity is released,** name where it should go, who owns that decision, who may authorize new demand against it, and a review date. Stopping work does not by itself mean next-cycle demand is covered: record whether it is Covered, Fragile, Not covered, or Unknown. Fragile needs a feasible contingency (a named person, a slot, the authority, and a trigger date); Unknown needs the missing evidence by a stated date; if either is not met, act as if Not covered and keep the recorded state. Then ask: **what happened to the capacity we stopped consuming?** (See [Release, protection, and redirection](#release-protection-and-redirection).)

**The simplest leadership test [H]:** *What should stop? What capacity will that release? Where should it go? What happened as a result?*

**Ask your team for:** the demand split into parts; the necessity evidence for each; the disposition chosen and the alternatives kept open; what is Unknown; the baseline and whether it is measured or estimated; the destination and owner of any release.

**Do not:** reduce a demand before asking whether it should exist; remove a control because it is slow; replace a mechanism before asking whether its demand is legitimate; treat saved time as usable capacity or as value. A disposition is a decision, not a result: the capacity it releases is not yet usable capacity, and what happens to it is a separate question.

What 5R has and has not shown is in the [evidence status](#status-and-evidence) and the [AKG relationship](#relationship-to-the-hee-case-studies-practice-record).

## The five dispositions

This table is the **canonical representation of the five dispositions in this document [H]**. The order of questions, the evidence-pattern table and the case restate or apply it; they are not additional or competing models.

| R | Governing question | Decision | Primary target |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **REMOVE** | Should this recurring demand exist at all? | Eliminate it | Existence |
| **REDUCE** | If it must exist, how can its consumption be reduced? | Reduce it | Consumption |
| **REPLACE** | Can the purpose be achieved through a simpler or different mechanism? | Substitute it | Method |
| **RE-ENGINEER** | Why does this demand keep arising? | Redesign the underlying condition | Recurrence |
| **RETAIN** | What necessary and valuable work should remain? | Clarify, own, and protect it | Justified demand |

**Replace or Re-engineer?** The two are the most easily confused.

| Response | Plain distinction | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Replace** | Change how a legitimate need is served | Replace payment-status calls with a reliable status view |
| **Re-engineer** | Change what generates the recurring demand | Correct invoice intake so repeated mismatch inquiries no longer arise |

A change can do both; record both rather than force a single label.

The five are **dispositions**, not five mandatory actions. A demand need not pass through all five. Each can apply to a distinct part of one demand, and a part can be both retained and reduced. 5R decides what should happen to a demand; it does not by itself carry out the change, release capacity, or create value.

### <a id="remove"></a>Remove

**Should this recurring demand exist at all?** Removal prevents the associated consumption entirely, so it is the first question. *Consider it when* the demand serves no purpose anyone can state; exists only because of historical practice; duplicates another activity; produces little or no useful outcome; exists only because another unnecessary activity exists; or can disappear without unacceptable risk or loss of value.

*Keep open:* a legal, safety, quality, or protective function; a hidden user; a purpose that is merely not recorded (which is **Unknown**, not “none”). Ask the named users and the control owner before concluding there is no use. Where the risk of removal is not clear, a **reversible trial** (suspend for a fixed period with a trigger to restore) may precede a permanent removal. **Trial rule:** a suspension is itself a change. The owner authorizes it only with documented reasons why the temporary interruption is acceptable, confirmation of the necessary safeguards, and monitoring suited to the potential harm, because “restore on request” alone cannot protect a use whose loss appears late. Examples below refer to this rule.

### <a id="reduce"></a>Reduce

**If the demand must exist, how can its consumption be reduced?** The purpose is legitimate but its frequency, volume, intensity, scope, complexity, or participation may exceed what it needs. *Levers:* fewer participants; fewer handoffs; fewer approval layers; lower reporting frequency; narrower scope; reduced duplication; simpler information requirements; lower intervention intensity.

*Keep open:* that lowering consumption may weaken the purpose it serves, and that a local saving may move the burden elsewhere (see *No Externalized Fire* under [Before changing anything](#before-changing-anything-necessity-and-legitimacy)). Check that the purpose is still met after the change, not only that effort fell.

### <a id="replace"></a>Replace

**Can the purpose be achieved through a simpler or different mechanism?** Replace changes the mechanism rather than optimizing the existing one. *Examples:* manual request → self-service; repeated request → standardized access; handoff → direct routing; recurring explanation → reusable knowledge; repeated approval → appropriate decision rights; automation where appropriate.

*Keep open:* that the new mechanism may create its own demand (upkeep, exceptions, training, a new dependency), may not be used, or may remove a useful control. Count the net demand after the replacement, including the mechanism’s own, before claiming a saving.

### <a id="re-engineer"></a>Re-engineer

**Why does this demand keep arising?** Re-engineering applies when a process, system configuration, policy, ownership model, dependency, information flow, decision-rights arrangement, interface, or upstream cause generates the demand again and again. The shift is from repeated intervention to **recurrence prevention**. [3C](https://github.com/titumozammel/3c-strategic-approach) can help ask where to look (what generates it, what it depends on, what restricts it); it does not establish the cause.

*Keep open:* that the apparent cause is not the real one, that the recurrence is legitimate, and that prevention may cost or risk more than continued response. Compare the cost and risk of prevention with those of continuing to respond, and treat a candidate cause as a hypothesis until evidence discriminates.

### <a id="retain"></a>Retain

**What necessary and valuable work should remain?** Some recurring demand is legally required, safety-critical, necessary for quality, customer service, risk management, governance, or learning, or simply proportionate to its value. Retain is a **decision**, not a default: it means naming the purpose, the owner, and the proportion, clarifying and standardizing the work, protecting it from being removed for the wrong reason, and setting a review date.

Retaining work because “we have always done it this way” is not retention; it is an unexamined demand. Retain can be reached at any point in the questions, not only at the end, and can combine with Reduce or Re-engineer (retain the purpose, redesign the means).

**Dispositions combine.** A weekly report might have seven sections removed, two retained, and its data feed re-engineered so the two need no hand collection. Label the parts, not the whole.

## Definitions and distinctions

These definitions follow the HEE Core README. Where 5R adds a term, it says so.

| Term | Working meaning | Practical distinction |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Demand | A requirement for work, action, attention, intervention, information, coordination, or decision | Not inherently negative; most demand is legitimate |
| Recurring demand | Demand that repeatedly arises or is repeatedly required | One-off work can consume capacity without a persistent burden |
| Unnecessary demand | Demand that does not need to exist, or does not need to exist in its present form | A judgment to be tested, not assumed from cost or frequency |
| Operational Noise | The operational manifestation of friction or unnecessary demand: avoidable interruption, duplication, rework, or coordination that consumes capacity without proportionate value | A symptom to trace to its underlying demand; not all demand or all consumption |
| Capacity | The part of capability that can be applied in a given period, given the time, attention, and energy available | Access, authority, and information belong to Enablement, not to capacity |
| Capacity consumption | Capacity used to meet demand | Consumption can be fully justified |
| Disposition (5R term) | The decision about what should happen to a recurring demand, or to a distinct part of it | A disposition can be chosen and never carried out, or carried out without producing the intended result |
| Capacity release | Capacity no longer consumed because demand has been removed or reduced | Measured, estimated, or merely expected; the three are different |
| Capacity protection | Arrangements that stop released capacity being silently consumed again | Not the same as refusing all new work |
| Usable Capacity Availability (OSF term) | Released capacity confirmed to be available for relevant work, with the required skill and access | Distinct from Capacity Recovery, Capacity Protection, and Human Energy Recovery; this entry concerns confirming whether released capacity is available for relevant work |
| Capacity redirection | Deliberate assignment of released capacity to another purpose | A justified reassignment is not a failure of protection |
| Capacity reabsorption [P] | Released capacity consumed again by other demand (new, latent, or existing) | A descriptive term. Reabsorption into a visible, justified reassignment is not a failure; the concern is unexamined or unjustified reabsorption (Core P13) |
| Application and Execution | Putting capability to work; carrying out intended work toward a result | Activity alone does not establish success or sustainability |
| Sustainable Execution Capacity (SEC) [P] | Continuing ability and readiness to execute effectively over time | Requires follow-up evidence; a faster task does not establish SEC |
| Value | Consequential outcomes across the six capitals: financial, manufactured, intellectual, human, social and relationship, and natural | Not identical to activity, output, or profit |

Therefore: **Demand ≠ Noise ≠ Consumption**, **Disposition ≠ Result**, and **Release ≠ Protection ≠ Availability ≠ Redirection ≠ Application ≠ Execution ≠ Value**. These are related conditions, not interchangeable words. Usable Capacity Availability is distinct from Capacity Recovery, Capacity Protection, and Human Energy Recovery.

## The Decision Point

*Quoted from the Core README’s The Decision Point (the canonical text, without its illustrative example). If the two differ, the Core README governs.*

Within HEE, consequential decisions along the pathway from capability to sustainable results should be explicit and reviewable. Evaluate them using four distinct definitions:

1. **Decision Enablement:** whether the necessary decision rights, information, authority, and access exist.
2. **Decision Process Quality:** how the decision was reached.
3. **Decision Quality:** whether the choice was appropriate given what could reasonably be known.
4. **Decision Outcome Quality:** what actually happened.

**What counts as a decision.** In HEE, a decision is a choice about what to do, including waiting or taking no action. A reviewable decision records its reasons, evidence, and uncertainty in proportion to the stakes. A choice made without such a record is still a decision, but HEE cannot fully assess its basis; add the reasons and evidence if you want it reviewed.

**Model boundary.** Decision Process Quality remains the core model’s conversion condition. These four definitions are complementary assessment lenses, not four additional conversion conditions or compulsory sequential stages.

Assess these separately. A favorable outcome does not prove that the process or choice was sound; an unfavorable outcome does not automatically establish that either was unsound. Context shapes every decision: its Time, Object, and Location, together with the legitimate demand, available evidence, constraints, alternatives, uncertainty, and consequences.

**The Two Checks.** Apply the Two Checks proportionately to decisions affecting capacity:

- **Justified?** Was the capacity spent warranted, considering purpose, necessity, proportionality and alternatives?
- **Covered?** Can credible expected usable capacity meet legitimate next-cycle demand?

A justified expenditure can still leave future capacity Not covered. Where evidence is insufficient, record Unknown and specify the next evidence request or precautionary action.

**Investigation routes.** Choose the route that fits the question:

- **Diagnose** (the Diagnostic Approach): investigate symptoms, test possible causes, and select a useful intervention.
- **Establish:** gather the evidence needed to make an uncertain question decidable.
- **Verify** (the Audit Approach): compare practice with a stated standard using traceable evidence.

These routes can inform one another; they are not compulsory sequential stages.

**The compressed conversion pathway and evidence boundaries.** Capability → Application → Execution → Value → Sustainable Growth. This compressed pathway does not replace the core model’s Human Energy, Connection, Decision Process Quality, Organizational Enablement, capacity, or renewal conditions. The Decision Point operates throughout this intended pathway. It makes choices and their consequences inspectable; it does not establish progression or sustainable results.

Customer Experience outcomes provide important evidence of customer-facing value. Sustainable Growth requires examining CX alongside employee experience, operating results, future-capacity coverage, and wider value consequences over time. Good CX alone does not establish that future delivery can be sustained.

**Applying the four definitions to a disposition choice.** Choosing what happens to a demand is itself a consequential decision. *Decision Enablement:* does the person choosing have the authority, information, and access to change the demand, or does someone else own the rule, the report, or the system? *Decision Process Quality:* were necessity and legitimacy examined, and the users and owners of the demand asked, before a disposition was chosen? *Decision Quality:* given what could reasonably be known, was the disposition appropriate? *Decision Outcome Quality:* what happened to the demand, to the capacity, and to the purpose the demand served? A removal that caused no harm does not prove the decision was well made (the purpose may simply not have been tested), and a restored report does not prove it was poorly made.

## What 5R contributes

5R proposes an order for considering dispositions, not a mandatory intervention sequence: *should it exist, can it consume less, can its purpose be met more simply, why does it keep arising, and what must stay?* It adds a disposition record that carries the choice through release, protection, destination, and follow-up. Its intended contribution is more inspectable disposition choices and a clearer separation between a change in activity and what happens to capacity, not the discovery that unnecessary work should be questioned. **3C = where to look; 5R = what to do about a recurring demand.** Neither requires the other.

Whether this order improves decisions, classification reliability, or outcomes beyond credible alternatives remains an empirical question. A new vocabulary or a successful example is not sufficient evidence of added value.

## One case from question to review

**All teams, quantities, thresholds, periods, and results below are invented [Illustrative]. This is neither a practice case nor evidence for 5R.**

An accounts-payable team receives supplier status inquiries. Leaders ask how to answer them faster. A better first question is: **why do suppliers have to ask, and which of these inquiries need a person?**

**Observation, without a cause.** Over six weeks the team logs about 90 inquiries a week, roughly 10 minutes each (about 15 hours a week). The tally is kept by the team; there is no system record, so the hours are *estimated*. A sample of 60 inquiries shows: 70% ask only for the payment date; 20% report a mismatched invoice detail (the same three fields each time); 10% are disputes that need judgment. A separate weekly status digest goes to 14 managers.

| Part of the demand | What the evidence holds | Candidate disposition |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Payment-date inquiries (70%) | The need to know is legitimate. The date is already in the system but not visible to the supplier, so the inquiry exists only because of the mechanism | **Replace:** an automatic payment notice or supplier-facing status view |
| Mismatched-detail inquiries (20%) | Recurs from the same three fields at invoice intake; the intake step has not changed | **Re-engineer:** validate those fields at intake |
| Disputes (10%) | Need judgment; responding resolves payment issues and protects the supplier relationship. A separate payment control also applies | **Retain:** give an owner and a service target; do not reduce yet. The control’s purpose and loss history are **Not recorded**, so **Unknown** until established |
| Weekly status digest | Two of the three managers asked say they do not use it; the third apparently does; the other 11 recipients were not asked | Ask the third manager and the 11 first; use and purpose are Unknown until then. Any interim suspension follows the [trial rule](#remove) |

**Alternatives still open.** Suppliers may still phone because they distrust a status view; a contract may require a written remittance advice; a digest recipient may use it in a way not yet known.

**Order and first reversible step.** Remove is asked first, but the digest cannot be removed on two “do not use it” answers out of fourteen: the third manager apparently uses it, and use and purpose for the other 11 recipients are Unknown. The preferred step is to ask the third manager and the 11; any interim suspension follows the [trial rule](#remove). The notice is piloted with 20 suppliers, with a stop rule written before it starts: if inquiries from the pilot group do not fall by a pre-set share within four weeks, or complaints rise, revert. The phone line and the dispute path are kept.

**Measures set before starting.** Inquiries per week by type; minutes per inquiry, timed on a sample of 20; intake mismatches; supplier complaints; against an eight-week baseline (the six-week tally above plus two further weeks collected before the pilot starts). Each figure is recorded as *measured* or *estimated*.

**Illustrative result.** After the notice is extended to all suppliers, inquiries fall from about 90 to about 50 a week (the fall is mostly date inquiries), and estimated effort from about 15 to about 9 hours a week. The observed reduction is consistent with the replacement reducing some payment-date inquiries; attribution remains uncertain without a suitable comparison, because other changes could explain part of it. The dispute portion was retained, and the purpose of its separate control remains Unknown until established. It does not show that six hours became usable capacity.

**What happens to the six hours.** The owner agrees in advance that they go to the intake fix. In the fourth week a new supplier-onboarding request arrives. Because the destination and owner were named, it goes through a visible reassignment decision rather than silent reabsorption: half is accepted and the rest deferred, with a review date.

**The Two Checks.** *Justified?* Some handling effort was warranted under the existing arrangement: staff needed to answer suppliers, and legitimate information needs do not by themselves show the effort was proportionate. The review identifies excess only where a feasible, proportionate alternative (a status view, intake validation) was available at the time, judged on what could reasonably have been known then; preventable recurrence alone does not make every response unjustified. On that basis some inquiries were warranted and part of the effort was excess, so the effort was **partly** justified. Responding to supplier disputes was warranted to resolve payment issues and protect the relationship; the separate control was retained precautionarily while its purpose and proportionality remained Unknown, and retention alone does not establish its justification. Without that review, justification would stay **Unknown**.

*Covered?* Next-cycle capacity on the intake fix is **Fragile**: it is covered in the base case but not if a plausible quarter-end close coincides with it. A credible contingency is named: a person, a scheduled slot, the authority to use it, and a trigger date. (See [Release, protection, and redirection](#release-protection-and-redirection) for what follows when capacity is released but the next cycle is not covered.)

**The four decision reviews.** The owner’s decision rights, authority, information and access support Decision Enablement. Suppliers’ access to the status view is a separate implementation condition. The sample, the questions asked, and the safeguards describe Decision Process Quality. The choice is judged against what was known before it began. Inquiry counts, complaints, and mismatches describe Decision Outcome Quality. These assessments may differ.

**What the example does not show.** It does not show that the order of questions produced a better decision than another method, that the replacement will keep working, that the intake fix will prevent the mismatches, or that the freed hours produced value. Those are claims to test.

## Choosing a disposition

**[H] A structured comparison of dispositions, not a validated procedure.** Use it to make the choice visible and reviewable. It is not a routing algorithm, and a demand need not pass through all five questions.

1. **State the demand without a cause or verdict:** what is requested or performed, by whom, how often, at what recorded cost, for what stated purpose.
2. **Test necessity and legitimacy first** (see [Before changing anything](#before-changing-anything-necessity-and-legitimacy)). Record what is **Unknown** and establish it before acting.
3. **Split the demand into parts** where purposes differ.
4. **Ask the five questions for each part,** in the order below.
5. **Compare feasible dispositions and combinations** by purpose served, total cost, expected benefit, implementation effort, risk, reversibility, and future-capacity consequences. Do not stop at the first feasible option. Where evidence is thin, prefer a reversible step with a stop rule written before it begins.
6. **Name safeguards and who is affected,** including where a burden might move.
7. **Before any release,** name the destination, the owner, and a review date.
8. **Review:** did the demand fall, did the purpose survive, where did the capacity go? Record what was learned.

| Order | Ask | If the evidence supports it | Why it is asked here | What would change the order |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Should this demand exist at all? | **Remove** (or trial removal) | Reducing, replacing, or re-engineering something that need not exist spends effort on it | A control with an unrecorded purpose: establish the purpose first. Any interim trial must meet the [trial rule](#remove), including authorization, safeguards and monitoring |
| 2 | If yes, can its consumption be reduced? | **Reduce** | Lowering the cost of a legitimate demand may be cheaper and less risky than a redesign, but compare the feasible options | Where the cost is driven by recurrence, re-engineering may come first |
| 3 | Can the purpose be met by a simpler mechanism? | **Replace** | Changing the mechanism can serve the purpose with less demand | A replacement that would carry a demand which should first be questioned |
| 4 | Why does it keep arising? | **Re-engineer** | Repeated handling of a recurring demand may be compensating for a design condition | Where the cause is cheap to fix and removal is uncertain, this may come earlier |
| 5 | What must remain and be protected? | **Retain** | Preserves justified demand and keeps it from being removed for the wrong reason | Can be reached at any point; combines with the others |

The order is a **sequence of questions to ask**, not a ranking of worth and not a claim about which disposition is best. Whether this order produces better choices than another is one of the [tests below](#research-agenda-and-tests).

| Evidence pattern | Candidate disposition | Useful next step |
| --- | --- | --- |
| No named purpose or user; named users confirm no continuing use or duty | Remove (candidate) | Suspend with a restore trigger under the [trial rule](#remove); watch for hidden users |
| Real purpose; effort exceeds what the purpose needs; parts carry no decision | Reduce | Cut the lever that lowers cost and keeps the purpose; check the purpose after |
| Real need; the mechanism creates the effort (manual requests for information the system holds) | Replace | Pilot the substitute; count the net demand including its upkeep |
| The same demand returns after it is handled; an upstream step creates it | Re-engineer | Trace the generating condition ([3C Source](https://github.com/titumozammel/3c-strategic-approach#choosing-a-context)); compare prevention cost with continued response |
| A duty or control with a documented purpose and proportionate effort | Retain | Name the owner, clarify the standard, set a review date |
| Purpose or cause not recorded | **Unknown** | Establish the next evidence by a stated date; do not act as if it were known |
| Work waits for access, authority, or a decision; or capability is absent | The primary problem lies outside 5R | Route to [HEEn](https://github.com/titumozammel/human-energy-enablement) or capability development; any recurring demand the problem generates can still be examined with 5R |
| Legitimate demand exceeds credible time or energy | A capacity shortfall; assess any coexisting drain separately | Protect or Recover; 5R may remove contributing demand but does not establish recovery |
| Parts point to different dispositions, or one part to several | A set of dispositions | Split into parts; record each part’s dispositions separately |

If both “outside 5R” patterns appear together, examine both explanations. Choose the next inquiry according to urgency, consequences, evidence gaps, authority, and reversibility; record and address each supported condition.


<a id="which-investigation-route"></a>**Which investigation route [H].** The routes come from [The Decision Point](#the-decision-point) and are detailed in [OSF](https://github.com/titumozammel/operational-silence-framework#investigate-choose-the-route); they can inform one another and are not compulsory stages.

| Route | Use it when | Typical 5R use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Diagnose** | The demand recurs and its cause is unclear | Test candidate causes before a Re-engineer; check the alternatives (capability shortage, blocked access, a capacity shortfall) |
| **Establish** | A purpose, baseline, or user is Unknown | Gather proportionate, decision-relevant evidence needed to resolve the uncertainty, with an owner and a date |
| **Verify** | A standard exists (a duty, a service commitment, a control) | Check that a retained control is performed, or that a safeguard held after a removal or replacement |

### How OSF and 5R fit together

OSF structures inquiry into recurring demand; 5R structures consideration of its possible disposition. Their question orders serve different purposes and do not form one compulsory sequence.

If necessity or the cause of recurrence is uncertain, use [OSF](https://github.com/titumozammel/operational-silence-framework) to investigate or establish the missing evidence. When enough is known to compare responses, use 5R: Remove, Reduce, Replace, Re-engineer, or Retain. Return to investigation whenever a proposed response exposes an unanswered question.

The 5R order is an order of consideration, not a ranking or a first-feasible rule. Re-engineer being fourth does not require trying the preceding options first. Compare feasible options and combinations against purpose, evidence, cost, risk, safeguards, and future-capacity consequences. Retain may be appropriate at any point.

## Before changing anything: necessity and legitimacy

5R treats a recurring demand as something to understand before it is changed. Before any disposition, including Retain, apply a proportionate purpose check; retention may continue temporarily as a precaution while an Unknown is established, and it is still recorded as Unknown, not as justified:

- **Apply the six tests** from [OSF](https://github.com/titumozammel/operational-silence-framework#diagnose-is-this-recurring-demand-and-is-it-unnecessary): Necessary? Valuable? Proportionate? Designed? Recurring? Preventable? In short: does the purpose require it, does it create enough value, is the amount proportionate, is it well designed, does it keep recurring, could upstream change prevent it? Record the evidence and any Unknown. 5R relies on that diagnosis; OSF holds the full version.
- **Use the constraint test in HEEn** ([test the constraint before changing it](https://github.com/titumozammel/human-energy-enablement#test-the-constraint-before-changing-it)) for any rule, approval, or control. A slow approval, a check, or a report may serve a legal duty, a safety purpose, or a protective function. Cost and frequency do not make it unnecessary.
- **Keep safeguards unless a replacement is shown.** If a safeguard is changed, name what takes over its protective purpose.
- **Allow Unknown.** If the purpose is not recorded, that is the finding; establish it before acting.
- **Check the two Core safeguards.** *Protective Friction* is intentional resistance that keeps low-value work from entering the system; it is a reason to design demand-entry criteria, not to remove every check. *No Externalized Fire* requires that a local saving not shift the burden to another team, function, or point in the process.
- **When resistance is political,** a defended rule is a reason to examine decision rights and incentives; it is not evidence that the rule is artificial. Use the Core README’s [bounded escalation protocol](https://github.com/titumozammel/human-energy-economics#when-a-constraint-is-politically-defended-h), including its guidance on when not to escalate.
- **Do not assume** that all recurring work is waste, that meetings are inherently harmful, that approvals are inherently unnecessary, that interruptions are inherently harmful, that fewer activities are always better, that automation is always beneficial, or that cost reduction equals capacity release. Operational Silence does not mean less communication.

## Questions before optimization

5R proposes changing the question before changing the activity. The table is illustrative **[H]**; every answer must be tested against evidence.

| Conventional question | 5R question |
| --- | --- |
| How can we do this faster? | Does this work need to exist? |
| How can we improve the meeting? | Should the meeting exist, and which part? |
| How can we process approvals faster? | Is the approval necessary, and for what? |
| How can we reduce rework? | What keeps creating the rework? |
| How do we manage exceptions? | Why do exceptions keep recurring? |
| How do we manage workload? | What demand is generating the workload? |
| How do we automate it? | Should the process exist in this form before we automate it? |
| How much time did we save? | What capacity was released, protected, and redirected? |
| How do we scale? | What demand is scaling with us unnecessarily? |

The principle is **question before optimizing**, a heuristic and not a law. Optimizing first can be reasonable when the stakes are low, the change is reversible, or the activity is plainly necessary.

**Simplify before automating [H].** Automation can improve the handling of necessary work. It does not by itself eliminate demand, simplify a process, prevent recurrence, release capacity, recover Human Energy, or create value. Do not automate complexity that should first have been questioned. Automation is a possible mechanism within Replace, not the objective of the framework.

**Prioritization [H].** The shared expression *Priority = Urgency × Interdependence × Value Impact* is a heuristic, not a validated equation or a meaningful numeric score without defensible scales. Use the factors alongside safety, authority, reversibility, uncertainty, and cost. **Interdependence** (how far relationships among people, functions, systems, and decisions affect movement or outcomes) is not the 3C **Dependency** context. Do not attack the largest demand automatically; weigh leverage, reversibility, and risk.

## Working principles

**[H] Heuristics, not laws.** Each holds unless evidence, stakes, or urgency say otherwise.

1. **Question before optimizing.**
2. **Prefer removal to reduction where the necessity test supports it:** reducing unnecessary work still leaves unnecessary work. This is a preference, not a rule; removing a control can be the riskier move, so a reversible trial may precede a removal.
3. **Ask reduce, replace, and re-engineer in that order where the evidence allows,** as an order of questions and not a ranking of worth.
4. **Re-engineer recurring causes** where prevention costs less than continued response.
5. **Retain what is necessary, valuable, and proportionate,** and review it.
6. **Simplify before automating.**
7. **Released capacity is not automatically available, redirected, applied, or valuable.**
8. **Protect released capacity,** and treat a visible, justified reassignment as a use, not a failure.
9. **Measure capacity, not merely activity,** and conversion, not merely reduction.
10. **Less activity does not automatically mean better performance.**
11. **Do not infer Human Energy recovery from demand reduction.**
12. **Prefer a reversible first step with a stop rule written beforehand.**
13. **Scale capability, not unnecessary demand** (a hypothesis to test).

## Release, protection, and redirection

A disposition changes a demand. What happens to capacity afterwards is a separate question, and each step needs its own evidence.

**Treatment → release → protection → availability → redirection → application → execution → value [P].** 5R acts at the front of this chain and establishes no later step. Less activity is not released capacity; released capacity is not usable capacity; usable capacity is not applied; applied capacity is not a result; a result is not value. The chain restates the economic consequence of a disposition; it is not a second 5R model, and it is not an automatic causal sequence.

**Justified or not, covered or not.** Not all consumption is loss; learning, maintenance, recovery, incident response, and deliberate investment can be justified. Apply the Two Checks to the consequences of a change. *Justified?* Was the capacity spent warranted (yes, partly, no, or Unknown, with the evidence)? *Covered?* Over a stated next-cycle horizon, does credible expected usable capacity meet legitimate demand? The evidence states are **Covered**, **Fragile**, **Not covered**, and **Unknown**. For **Fragile**, require a credible contingency (a named person, a scheduled slot, the resource or authority, and a trigger date); if any is missing or infeasible, retain Fragile in the record and act precautionarily as if Not covered. For **Unknown**, establish the missing evidence by a stated date, and if it passes without decisive evidence, retain Unknown in the record and act as if Not covered for the decision. Precautionary action does not change the recorded state. Removing unnecessary work does not by itself establish that the next cycle is covered. See [OSF’s release and coverage section](https://github.com/titumozammel/operational-silence-framework#justified-expenditure-release-and-future-coverage) for the full table and the Core’s [one-page card](https://github.com/titumozammel/human-energy-economics#justified-spending-or-future-shortfall-one-page-card-hp).

**Protect the gain [H].** Follow the Core README’s [capacity protection and anti-reabsorption protocol](https://github.com/titumozammel/human-energy-economics#capacity-protection-and-anti-reabsorption-protocol-h): baseline the activity; verify that the work or resource use actually fell, marking it *measured* or *estimated*; name the intended use before release; assign an accountable owner and say who can authorize new demand against it; make any reallocation an explicit decision; and review across cycles at a pre-set date. Protection mechanisms include ownership, decision rights, standards, governance, documentation, controls, capability transfer, monitoring, review, and recurrence detection. The protocol does not give a practitioner authority to refuse legitimate new work.

**Reabsorption checks.** After a disposition, ask: what new demand appeared; what existing demand expanded; did the workload merely move; did another function absorb it; did new reporting or coordination appear; did replacement complexity arise? *Activity removed ≠ capacity permanently released.* Record a justified reassignment separately from silent reabsorption into equivalent low-value work. Whether protection changes reabsorption is an empirical question (Core P5 and P13).

**Redirection is a management decision.** Released capacity may go to customer-facing work, strategic priorities, quality, risk reduction, execution bottlenecks, capability development, learning, resilience, or future capacity. The logic is *release, protect, deliberately redirect*, not *release, assume value*. **Capacity Protection ≠ Human Energy Recovery:** Restoring depleted capacity belongs to the Core’s *Recover* intervention; [HERF](https://github.com/titumozammel/human-energy-economics) specifically addresses Human Energy recovery. Where capability exists but organizational conditions prevent its application, route to [HEEn](https://github.com/titumozammel/human-energy-enablement); execution management belongs to [EES](https://github.com/titumozammel/execution-excellence-system).

**Units and horizons.** Keep units, skills, horizons, and demand assumptions consistent. A saved hour of one skill does not cover a shortfall in another. Reduced waiting does not necessarily release equivalent person-hours, and a faster step can expose a downstream bottleneck.

**The shared chain of questions.** For what happened after a disposition, use the [OSF Value Test](https://github.com/titumozammel/operational-silence-framework#the-osf-value-test): ten steps from the work that disappeared to value across the relevant capitals, with the rule to **stop at the last supported step**. 5R adds one step before it: *what was the disposition, and on what evidence?* Do not attribute a later result to a step that is unverified.

## The disposition record

**[H] A short record for each demand.** It makes the choice reviewable and carries it through to follow-up. It is a recording aid, not a validated instrument.

| Field | What to record |
| --- | --- |
| Demand and parts | What recurs, for whom, how often, at what recorded cost; the parts, if purposes differ |
| Necessity evidence | Answers to the six tests for each part, with the source and what is Unknown |
| Disposition and alternatives | The chosen disposition for each part and the alternatives still open |
| Safeguards and stop rule | What is kept, who is affected, what burden might move, and the stop rule written beforehand |
| Baseline | Frequency, volume, effort, and recurrence, each *measured* or *estimated*, with the period |
| Release | What fell, and what capacity is released (measured, estimated, or merely expected) |
| Protection and destination | Named use, owner, who may authorize new demand against it, and the review date |
| Reabsorption | What arrived since the change; reassignments recorded as decisions |
| Justified? | Yes, Partly, No, or Unknown, with the evidence |
| Covered? | Covered, Fragile, Not covered, or Unknown, for the stated horizon; the recorded state is kept even when action is precautionary |
| Follow-up | Application, execution, and next-cycle results observed, each on its own evidence |

**Measures.** The principle is to *measure what became possible, not merely what disappeared*. Observations follow the chain.

| Stage | Possible observation |
| --- | --- |
| Demand | Frequency, volume, recurrence |
| Noise | Unnecessary operational activity or friction |
| Consumption | Hours, attention, processing effort, interventions |
| Release | Capacity no longer consumed |
| Protection | Degree to which return of demand was prevented |
| Redirection | Destination of the released capacity |
| Application | Actual use of the released capacity |
| Execution | Results produced |
| Future capacity | Strengthened ability to execute |
| Value | Customer, strategic, operational, or financial effect |

Record each as *measured*, *estimated*, or *merely expected*, and keep the three apart.

## Growth, experience, and service commitments

**Growth and complexity.** Growth commonly adds decisions, approvals, coordination, interfaces, handoffs, reporting, meetings, dependencies, and exceptions. As complexity grows, demand may increase faster than usable capacity, and an organization may have more capability while having less usable capacity to apply it. Growth is not the problem, and some complexity is necessary; the question is which demand creates value and which consumes capacity without it. This is a hypothesis to test, not a finding.

| Topic | What 5R may help examine | What 5R does not establish |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Capacity | Recurring demand that consumes capacity without proportionate value | That released capacity is protected, usable, or applied |
| Human Energy | Demand associated with interruption, rework, and unnecessary coordination | That Human Energy has recovered; demand reduction is not recovery |
| Employee Experience | Operating conditions that may affect working conditions indirectly | That experience will improve; 5R is not employee experience management |
| Customer Experience | Internal recurring demand that may sit behind delay, error, repeat contact, or inconsistent service | That a negative CX result proves an internal demand; a signal is not proof |
| SLA and XLA | Demand that may consume capacity needed to sustain service and experience commitments | That a missed commitment is caused by that demand; 5R does not replace SLA or XLA management |

**How a disposition may reach experience and growth [P].** A disposition may change employee workload, interruptions, and waiting; those operating conditions may change what capacity can be applied; and application may reach customer delivery. Each link needs its own evidence, and a change at one does not establish a change at the next. Sustainable growth requires examining customer experience alongside employee experience, operating results, and future-capacity coverage over time; good customer experience alone does not establish that delivery can be sustained, and 5R does not supply that evidence.

## Boundaries and architecture

HEE is the capacity lens informed by economic questions. **OEOS** (Organizational Excellence Operating System) is the operating architecture; **HEMS** (Human Energy Management System), **HEA** (Human Energy Audit), **HERF** (Human Energy Recovery Framework) and **HEDP** (Human Energy Development Plan) are Human Energy and capability-management components documented within the Core. These are proposed constructs, not validated systems. 5R is a decision method for one kind of choice. It is not an operating system, a diagnostic condition, or an intervention in the Core’s sense.

| Related element | Its role | 5R boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| HEE Core | The capacity lens, diagnostic vocabulary, Two Checks, and research tests | The Core governs shared terms (its §10 relates 5R to the five interventions); 5R does not restate them as its own |
| OSF | Whether recurring operational demand should exist, how it is diagnosed, and what happens to the capacity released | 5R is the disposition method inside OSF’s logic. OSF owns the necessity diagnosis, the Operational Silence Index, and the Value Test; 5R does not duplicate them |
| 3C | Where to look: Source, Dependency, or Constraint | **3C = WHERE; 5R = HOW.** The relationship is complementary: not every 5R choice needs a prior 3C, and 3C is not a mandatory first stage. A signal is not a diagnosis |
| HEEn | Enabling existing capability to be applied | 5R selects the demand disposition; implementation may change enabling conditions (for example, replacing an approval with decision rights) and then involves HEEn. The two answer different questions but can apply to the same change |
| HEL | A systemic orientation for recurring artificial restrictions; not a sixth intervention | 5R works at the level of one demand; systemic patterns may need the broader lens |
| HEMS / HEA / HERF | Manage, audit, and recover Human Energy | 5R does not recover Human Energy |
| HEDP | Develops genuinely missing capability | 5R cannot supply a skill that is absent |
| Capability Interconnection | Connects distributed capability for application | 5R may remove an unnecessary dependency; it does not connect capability |
| EES | Manages execution through Find, Fix, Control, Adapt, Grow | 5R does not manage execution |

**How 5R relates to the Core’s five interventions.** The 5R Cascade determines what should happen to a demand. The Core’s five interventions (Protect, Recover, Develop, Connect, Enable) address the capacity and organizational conditions needed for effective application. They answer different questions and are not one combined sequence; Remove, Reduce, and the other Rs are dispositions, not the Core’s interventions. Where the Core must distinguish a capacity shortfall from a drain, it uses 5R on a sample to test whether a demand is necessary.

Some elements are documented within the Core and have no separate repository. Markets, strategy, regulation, technical failures, industrial relations, and health questions may need other explanations or specialists. 5R is **not** a mandate to reduce activity, a cost-reduction program, a replacement for lean, process improvement, or failure-demand analysis, or a method for deciding enterprise strategy.

## Descriptive progression (legacy construct, not tested)

| Level | Prompt | What it describes |

| 1. Reactive | “Fix what is happening.” | Attention on visible problems and immediate consequences |
| 2. Aware | “This keeps happening.” | Recurrence is recognized |
| 3. Challenging | “Should this work continue?” | Recurring demand is questioned |

## Relationship to the HEE Case Studies practice record

Readers are invited to recognize HEE’s questions and reasoning in the author’s practice cases, consider what fits their own context, and apply it with proportionate evidence and judgment.

The [HEE Case Studies](https://github.com/titumozammel/hee-case-studies) gives eight examples from the author’s experience (one at RR Transport, one at Bismillah Transport, and six at AKG). HEE was not named when they happened. The author maps them in retrospect to dispositions: one to Replace (RDP / Restart), one to Reduce (Performance Wizard), three to Re-engineer (Hardware Health; Report; RR Transport, which also replaced manual books with an application and reduced roadside breakdowns), one to Re-engineer and Retain (Procurement, a retain-and-redesign case and not a drain-removal case), and two to Enable (Capability; Bismillah Transport’s vehicle-wise profit and loss; enablement cases rather than 5R dispositions). **None of the eight is documented as a Remove case.**

That is a retrospective reading. The record does not document the necessity tests applied, the alternative dispositions considered, who classified each case, or where any released capacity went beyond one author-reported use (continuing user growth in the support cases, Cases 3 and 5; more trips in RR Transport, Case 1); elsewhere it is **Not recorded** and therefore **Unknown**. The figures are author-reported and not independently verified, and the record was not designed to test 5R. Where another document lists the dispositions with arrows, read them as an order of questions, not a sequence every demand must pass through.

Do not use the cases to claim that the five dispositions are distinct, that assessors can classify them reliably, that the order of questions improves decisions, or that any released capacity became value. They can motivate a study; they cannot validate 5R. The record says only that the author reports changes in demand, and it treats them as author-reported.

The author remains Chief Innovation Officer at AKG. His operational involvement and originator role are material to interpreting the practice accounts. Research use requires appropriate authority, confidentiality, provenance, and access arrangements; no access is implied here.

## Related approaches

5R's questions are close to well-known approaches: lean waste reduction, business process reengineering, failure-demand analysis, and the habit of asking whether work should be done before improving how it is done. 5R does not claim these ideas are new, and it has not been compared with them in a fair test. Use 5R together with any of them where they fit.

5R adds a narrow claim **[P]**: an order for considering the five dispositions for a recurring demand (not a mandatory sequence), a legitimacy check, Retain as a full disposition, and a record that carries the choice through protection, destination, and coverage. This claim is untested. If the five dispositions cannot be told apart or classified reliably, 5R is best used as a set of question prompts.

The comparison questions and unverified reading leads are in [Research notes](https://github.com/titumozammel/human-energy-economics/blob/HEAD/RESEARCH_NOTES.md#related-approaches-and-comparison-questions). They are not evidence for 5R.

## Status and evidence

**What 5R is.** The 5R Cascade Framework is HEE’s practice-driven method for choosing what should happen to a recurring operational demand once its necessity has been examined: Remove, Reduce, Replace, Re-engineer, or Retain. It does not establish that its order of questions improves decisions, that the five dispositions are distinct and can be classified reliably, that released capacity becomes usable capacity, or that any downstream result follows. Those are questions for evidence.

**Evidence status.** 5R is a [practice-driven](https://github.com/titumozammel/human-energy-economics#4-core-definitions) framework, derived from the author’s practice. Its five dispositions, its order of questions, and the capacity chain that follows a disposition are proposals that require testing. Established work on lean waste reduction, process redesign, and failure demand overlaps with the framework in places; it does not validate 5R’s particular ordering or show that 5R adds decision value beyond those methods. The author originated HEE and 5R and has a commercial interest disclosed below. The cases in HEE Case Studies are examples from the author’s experience (author-reported) and are not tests of 5R. The [HEE Core README](https://github.com/titumozammel/human-energy-economics) governs shared terminology where the documents differ.

**Foundation.** HEE comes from the author’s practice. The [HEE Core README](https://github.com/titumozammel/human-energy-economics) sets out its foundation and approach. In 5R this shows as choosing the disposition that suits each demand in its context and reviewing whether the demand recurs.

**How to read the status of 5R**

- **Labels:** **[H]** marks a practice heuristic, a rule of thumb from practice. **[Illustrative]** marks an invented example, not reported evidence. **[P]** marks a specific construct, relationship, or claim that remains untested. Labels are used sparingly: practical instructions and HEE’s practice origin carry none. A label describes the status of a claim, not its certainty in every setting.
- **Unknown and Not recorded:** **Unknown** means the available evidence does not support a defensible determination. **Not recorded** means this edition contains no record; it does not mean the event never occurred or that no record exists elsewhere.
- **What would change 5R:** see [Open questions](#research-agenda-and-tests). What would narrow or retire 5R is in [Research notes](https://github.com/titumozammel/human-energy-economics/blob/HEAD/RESEARCH_NOTES.md#5r-research-agenda-and-tests).
- **What 5R is not:** not independently validated, not shown to be better than lean waste reduction, process redesign, or failure demand analysis, and not a clinical diagnostic tool or a substitute for applicable compliance requirements or professional audit standards.
